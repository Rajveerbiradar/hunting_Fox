## What it is

Hunting Fox helps Indian college students get noticed for a job. A student pastes a LinkedIn or Naukri job link and gets:

1. A check that the company is real (not fake / closed / agency).
2. What to change in their resume for that job (only skills they confirmed they have).
3. People who work there (public LinkedIn data), ranked by who's most likely to reply.
4. Each person's LinkedIn link and any work email we found. **We don't write messages or LinkedIn notes** (removed 19 Nov 2026: the AI emails weren't good enough). The student writes and sends their own. We never send anything.

It's Rajveer's free personal project, built for Beep's Founder's Office task (deadline Fri 20 Nov 2026). **No search without a resume** (`POST /api/jobs` answers 409 `no_resume`): the page saves the link (`hf_pending_link`) and shows `NeedResume`, the sign-in style screen "Your job link is saved here · Upload your resume to start" with the upload card in a moving brand outline; the setup page then offers "Start the search". The sign-in page says under the Google button: "Then upload your resume to start the search." **Every student gets 5 searches the moment they sign in** (20 Nov 2026; `FREE_SEARCHES` in `src/lib/free.ts`, applied in `access()` in `src/lib/limits.ts`: anyone not declined is approved and raised to at least 5). More come from the admin. The first time a student lands on Home, a popup (`SearchesWelcome`, once per account per browser via localStorage) says they've got 5 searches and to ask Rajveer for more on WhatsApp, email or a call, expecting a reply in 1–3 hours.

## How a search runs (server side)

`POST /api/jobs` (src/app/api/jobs/route.ts) checks approval + limit + "paused", inserts a `jobs` row, then `after(() => runJob(id))` so it runs in the background in its own invocation. The browser only polls.

`runJob` → `scan` in `src/lib/pipeline/run.ts`:

0. Read job (LinkedIn/Naukri actor) — `fetchers.ts`
1. Check company — `rules.ts` `checkCompany` (fake / closed / agency stop here; they don't count as a search). A website listed on the LinkedIn page is only "unknown", not proof. After step 2: **0 people + no visible poster + page says 51+ employees** → stops as `fake` with `error = no_people` (no resume work, doesn't count). 0 or 1 person + no poster at a smaller company → a warning check and verdict `unverified`, but the search carries on
2. Find people — `fetchPeople`: **from the company's own LinkedIn employee list** (`harvestapi/linkedin-company-employees` by the company's LinkedIn URL, "Full + email search"), **no job-title filter**: up to 15 people (8 in lean mode). Companies with 201+ people also get a second run at the same time with `searchQuery` = the job's main word (10 / 5 more), so the closest people aren't lost among thousands. Nobody is filtered out by role: the rank step orders everyone by how close they are to the job (Founder's Office → founders/CEO first; design → design lead first). **An empty employee run is never trusted** (20 Nov: NextGen Digital Solutions, 51–200 people, came back with 0 in 3 s and was called fake; a second run found people): when "everyone" returns 0, it waits 2 s and runs once more in Short mode (names + titles, no emails) before the 0-people rule can stop the search. Only when the job has no LinkedIn company page (some Naukri jobs): b2bsearch by website, with every person cross-checked (`worksThere`). Emails kept only on the company's domain (or any non-free-mail domain when the domain is unknown). A work email is only a small tie-breaker in the ranking (closeness to the job leads). Cached per company **and job title** for 7 days under `#v4#` (empty results aren't reused), minus `blocked_people`
3. Resume changes ∥ people ∥ rank. The resume work (`makeResume` in `src/lib/pipeline/resume.ts`, prompt `prompts/resume-changes.md`) **starts right after the company check, in parallel with the people search**, and is awaited with the ranking. It first works out what the company believes and is building (from `company_data.about/tagline/specialties`, taken from the job scraper's company block, plus the job post's "About us") and what the student would deliver there (`company_brief`, shown on the resume page); the summary's last sentence names the company and that plan, and never opens with a past employer. Step 3b in both prompts thinks like the person deciding whether to hire: anything that makes them hesitate is reframed (never "Founder" as the identity when applying to join a company, e.g. Founder's Office; their own product is proof, not identity; no overselling for intern roles; one clear identity matching the job). It reads the job like a recruiter: 3–5 `priorities` in the job's own language with the student's proof for each (strong/weak/none), then rewrites the headline, summary (3 sentences, 45–70 words, bold) and every supporting bullet in that language, only from real facts (resume lines + `profiles.facts` + skills confirmed at level 2+). Not keyword stuffing; **change by default** (`perfect_match` is rare). Weak/missing priorities become `gaps` (up to 4 open questions, ids `g0…`); unconfirmed tools become `ask` → `skill_questions`. Code drops any edit that adds a known tool word the student hasn't got, and never re-asks skills they said no to or gaps already answered. Saved as `resume.v = 2`, `applied: false`
   **Skills from the people we found** (20 Nov): the people page (`ReachOut`) shows "Skills people at {company} list" (`#skills`, top 16 **relevant to this job** via `relevantSkills` in `src/lib/skillMatch.ts`: a skill counts only if the job post names it, or it comes from someone whose title is in the same work family as the job title (developer ≈ engineer ≈ SDE; Founder's Office ≈ chief of staff, but not the founders themselves); post-named skills first, tagged "IN THE JOB POST") and each picked person's skills under their title (for someone in a different kind of work, a note says so; every skill, related or not, can still be added). Skills already on the student's resume or confirmed at level 2+ are green ✓; the rest have a small **+** in front of the name → `resume-plan {action:"add_skill", skill, from}` adds it to this job's `skill_questions` (why: "<first name> at this company lists it"), so the resume page asks how well they know it and a confirmed one goes into the skills line. The resume page links there ("See what people at {company} know", a card with a fast-moving brand-gradient outline, `.glow` in globals.css, so students notice it; off with reduced motion). We match skills, not roles: we don't rewrite the resume from employee profiles, because a profile only helps when it's the exact same role, and the student judges what's true.
4. Ready — the job is marked **ready right after step 3** (report saved with the ranked people). **No messages are written.** `/jobs/[id]/reach-out` is the "People at {company}" page (`ReachOut`): the ranked list; for the picked person their title and the rank's one-line why, **View on LinkedIn**, the work email (Copy, Open in Gmail) when found, an "I contacted them" toggle (`jobs.sent_to`), and a Download PDF button to attach the resume. The hiring inbox from the job post shows too. Message writing (`writePerson`, `POST /api/jobs/[id]/more`, `prompts/write-messages.md`) was **deleted**; see GOTCHAS 10i. Old reports may still hold `note`/`subject`/`body` on people; they're ignored.
5. `events` "finished" is written when the job turns ready

`jobs.step` (0–5) + `step_detail` drive the progress popup, the live "three dots" step on Home/My jobs and admin Jobs. Job lists use `useLiveJobs` (ui.tsx): a new search shows up at once (`announceJob()` fires `hf:jobs` when a search starts; the hook pulls `/api/jobs` and adds new rows on top), running ones poll every 2.5 s, and a server refresh replaces the list.

Student's own keys (`src/lib/ownKeys.ts`): if they have an Apify key, the search runs on it only and `jobs.own_key = true` (doesn't count). If it runs out: fall back to free searches or stop (`user_keys.fallback`).

Apify rotation (`src/lib/apify.ts` `runActor`): DB tokens in `added_at` order, then env. Rotates on 401/402/403/credit text and on 429/5xx/network; parks exhausted accounts; **never** retries a timeout on another account (it may still be running and costing money). Pooled credit = sum of all accounts; below $5 → "lean" mode.

Ranking people with **Clef** (`src/lib/clef.ts`): Cloudflare's decision model (`@cf/cloudflare/clef-flash` or `clef`, REST `/ai/run`, System One API: `state` + typed `questions` → probabilities, wrapped in `result`). It's not a chat model, so it's not in the AI providers list. Set up in /admin → API keys → Clef (Account ID + Workers AI token + model, tested before save; stored as settings `clef_account_id`, `clef_token`, `clef_model`). When `ranker_provider` = `clef`, `rankTask` asks Clef one `score` question (how close the person is to this job: who runs or does this work first, unrelated functions last) and one `choice` question (real connection) per person, re-checks every connection in code (exact school / company / skill / "hiring" in headline), and builds the one-line "why" from templates. Any Clef failure falls back to the chat ranker (`decide`).

AI (`src/lib/ai.ts`): one call per task, JSON mode with fallback. **When a provider is picked for a task** (`writer_provider` / `ranker_provider`, Admin → API keys) **only that one is used** (plus a student's own key first); it never falls back to another model. With nothing picked, every provider is tried in order. The admin dropdown shows "Not picked: try each AI in order" when unset (it used to display the first AI as if picked, so choosing it saved nothing). A failed task's error lists every attempt in order (`Tried your own key: … · then Anthropic (claude-sonnet-5-5), 12s: …`). Temperature and JSON mode are optional extras: if a model rejects one with a 400 (Sonnet 5.5: "`temperature` is deprecated for this model"), `chat` drops the one the error names, asks again, and remembers it for that model. Every call is **streamed** (`stream: true`): the reply builds up piece by piece, so long replies are never killed by a request timeout. **No caps**: every call may write up to the model's own output limit (`OUT` = 32,000, or the lower number a model's 400 names); you pay only for what's written. No total-time cap: it gives up only if nothing starts within 60 s. Length is controlled by the prompts (e.g. the LinkedIn note's 200 characters is a strict prompt rule; code never cuts it). Errors name the provider, model and seconds (`Anthropic (claude-sonnet-5-5), 66s: Request timed out`), and that's what the job log shows (students see only "Our AI couldn't respond. Try again."). A reply that reaches the model's limit, a timeout, or a reply that isn't JSON is **not** retried on the same provider (no paying twice) (it would only repeat); only network/408/429/5xx are. Every call logs its time and size in the job log (`… OK in 42.3s, 9120 characters`).

**Two-step writing** (`writerBuilt` in ai.ts): for resume changes (the only writing task now), call 1 runs `prompts/build-prompt.md`: it reads the job post, the company page data from the job listing (often nearly empty), the student's resume, notes and confirmed skills. **It does not see the people we find**: the resume starts in parallel with the people search plus the task's own prompt file as the rules, and writes a fresh, detailed system prompt (Morning Espresso structure, ~700–1,500 words) for this one task. Call 2 follows that prompt. No two prompts are the same; the email has no fixed paragraph order (it tells the student's real story). Costs two calls per task.

**AI budgets** (`src/lib/usage.ts`): every AI call (and Clef) is recorded in `ai_usage` with tokens from the provider's streamed `usage` (or counted from text length, marked `estimated`). Unless the student used their own key, tokens are added to `profiles.ai_tokens_used`; `checkBudget()` stops before any call once it reaches `ai_token_limit` (`AiBudgetError`). Students never see token numbers: Account shows "AI credits · N% left" as a bar; at 10% left a warning (Account and Home) tells them to ask the admin early; at 0 they see "The AI budget allocated to you is finished. AI credits are separate from your search credits…". Admin → Students shows each student's AI use with +100k / +250k top-ups (a top-up sets the limit to max(limit, used) + the amount, because usage can run past the limit; adding to the old limit once left a student still blocked). The job resume page shows the old "budget finished" message only while the budget is still finished (`budgetDone` from `budgetLeft`). Admin → API keys → "AI usage and cost": one box per model (and Clef), tokens in/out, calls and cost for 30 days and all time, with a price per million tokens to enter; a removed model keeps its box for 30 days. Students see only "Our AI couldn't respond. Try again." (or the budget message) when AI fails; the real reason is in the job log.

## Debugging — start here

- **/admin → Jobs → Log** on any job: every step, every Apify account tried (status, ms), every AI call, and the error + stack. Same lines in Vercel → Logs, prefixed `[job xxxxxxxx]`. Logging is `jlog()` in `src/lib/joblog.ts` (AsyncLocalStorage carries the job id).
- `jobs.error` holds the short reason; the admin Jobs list shows it in red.
- Common causes:
  - `No Apify tokens` → add an Apify account in /admin → API keys.
  - `No AI provider` → add a provider in /admin → API keys. (Resume upload also needs this: it returns `no_ai`.)
  - Sign-in loops / lands signed out → Supabase redirect URLs, or Google OAuth client redirect URI `https://bordbhjvnstmvjcicrhy.supabase.co/auth/v1/callback`.
  - `/admin` says "This account isn't an admin" → email not in `ADMIN_EMAILS` / Settings, or not redeployed.
- "Run again" in admin Jobs retries without using the student's search.
- **Open** in admin Jobs opens any student's job pages as they see them (`loadJob` falls back to the service key for admins, with the student's own profile, and `AdminViewBar` says it's a read-only admin view; buttons that change things still check the owner and won't work). It used to 404 for jobs that weren't the admin's own.
- **Results** in admin Jobs (`GET /api/admin/jobs?result=<id>`, `ResultPanel` in AdminJobs.tsx): exactly what the student got: the company checks and verdict, the resume work (company brief, priorities with proof strength, every change old → new with its reason, dropped ones faded, applied or not, failures), the questions and skills asked, and the ranked people with title, why, email, LinkedIn and skills. Use it to review quality on real students' jobs.
- **Test bench: `POST /api/admin/test`** (admin session only). `{action:"resume", jobId?, rules?, builder?}` runs the real resume rewrite with the keys saved in /admin on a job (default: the admin's newest ready job) and **saves nothing**. `rules` / `builder` are optional prompt texts that replace `prompts/resume-changes.md` / `prompts/build-prompt.md` for that run, so a prompt can be tested before it's deployed. `draft: "v4"` loads `prompts/drafts/resume-changes-v4.md` (and `build-prompt-v4.md` if present) instead, so drafts can be committed and tested without pasting them; nothing else reads `prompts/drafts/`. From v8: a rules file starting with `<!-- APPEND-RULES -->` is appended word for word after the brief the first step writes (so the builder writes only a brief), and `writerBuilt` takes a `check` function: if code finds problems (`pageProblems`), the writer gets one more call to fix exactly those; bullet endings are made consistent in code (`evenEndings`). From v9: no assumed reader (write for a cold professional who knows nothing about the student); the rules' "warm up before you claim" step and the builder's setup map make every name, number and claim get set up first, in the same sentence or an earlier line. `makeResume` also accepts a **full-page answer** (`lines`: the whole resume, same length and order, plus `reasons` by line index), which draft v6 uses so no line can be skipped; it's turned into edits, and a line-count mismatch is logged and ignored. Up to 40 edits are kept. It runs in the background (a browser request drops after ~90 s): POST answers `{run}`, then `GET /api/admin/test?run=<id>` gives `{status:"running"}` or the result: `built_prompt`, `raw`, `before`, `after` (all changes applied). Results are saved as JSON in the `resumes` bucket under `test-runs/`. Logs go to that job's log; tokens show in AI usage and cost but never count against any student budget (the admin's included). Call it from a signed-in browser: `fetch('/api/admin/test',{method:'POST',headers:{'content-type':'application/json'},body:JSON.stringify({action:'resume'})})`.

## Resume template

Upload (`/api/resume`): POST checks the file, pulls the text, stores the file, sets `pending_resume_profile = {status:"reading"}` and answers at once (202). The AI read runs in `after()` and **saves it as the student's resume straight away** (no confirm step), leaving `{status:"done", missing}` or `{status:"failed", error}` in `pending_resume_profile`. GET returns that status + current lines; `ResumeUpload` reads it on every page open and polls every 3 s while reading, so the student can leave. When it's done, the card clears the notice (DELETE) and opens `/resume/setup?new=1`. A read stuck over 4 min shows as failed.


**Resume setup** (`/resume/setup`, `ResumeSetup.tsx`, `src/lib/resumeGroups.ts`, `POST /api/resume/setup`): the student checks the main resume one part at a time (the top, the summary, every `### ` entry, every section without entries such as Skills). Each part asks "Is this written right? Anything to add or change?": **Yes, it's right** marks it checked; otherwise the student writes rough notes and **Update this part** sends that one part + the note to `prompts/resume-section-fix.md` (the note is the truth: false claims go, their facts are added, untouched lines stay identical), saves the new lines into `profiles.resume_text`, and keeps the note in `profiles.facts` as `About "<part>" on my resume` (those notes never roll off the 40-fact cap), so every future job reads it. Undo puts the old lines back. The page sends the lines it saw; if the resume changed since, the answer is 409 and the page reloads. Checked/updated state is `resume_profile.setup[key]` (no migration). **After an upload is read, the student goes straight there** (`/resume/setup?new=1`, no "Looks good" step) and a popup says: change anything the way you like, don't worry about spelling or perfect wording, we'll make it a well-written resume (plus "Worth adding" from `resume_profile.missing`). **Mandatory sections** (`MANDATORY` in resumeGroups.ts: Summary, Experience, Projects, Education, Skills) always have a place: a missing one shows as a dashed "{Section}: nothing here yet" card with an open text box, and every Experience / Projects / Education section ends with "+ Add another to {Section}". Both send `{action:"add", section, note}`: the AI writes the new part from the note alone (`mode:"add"` in the prompt) and `insertInSection` puts it at the end of that section, or creates the section in the usual order. **Save and finish** and the job resume page's **Apply** both warn first when a mandatory section is missing (`MissingWarn`: Add them now / Save or Apply anyway, with a "Don't warn me again" checkbox kept in localStorage). A job link saved before the resume was in shows at the top as "Your job link is saved · Start the search". Also linked from Account. Why: the saved resume is the source for every job, and an AI-written line on it (e.g. "AI CRM") became a "fact" forever (GOTCHAS 10n).

Every resume is drawn by `ResumeDoc` (src/components/ResumeDoc.tsx) on screen: upload check, the job's resume page (one block, "Show changes" / "Final"), and the shared link (`/r/[token]`). **PDFs are made in the student's browser** (like OpenResume), not on the server and not with the print dialog: `PdfButton` (src/components/PdfButton.tsx) dynamic-imports `src/lib/resumePdf.tsx` (`@react-pdf/renderer`, Reddit Sans from `public/fonts/`), draws the same lines in the same layout and saves "Name - Resume - Company.pdf". The resume page passes its own lines (so the latest skill answers are in it); elsewhere the button fetches them from `GET /api/jobs/[id]/resume-lines` (owner only) or `GET /api/r/[token]/lines` (shared link). We tried server-side `renderToBuffer` first: it worked locally but failed on Vercel, so don't move it back. The old `/jobs/[id]/resume/print` redirects to the resume page. No Chrome date/title/URL/page-number lines. On upload `prompts/resume-profile.md` returns the resume as **structured JSON** (name, headline, contact, summary, experience, projects, education, skills, achievements, other; text fields are markdown with `**bold**`), and `toLines` in `src/lib/resumeFormat.ts` turns it into lines in a fixed order (contact: email · phone · city · LinkedIn · GitHub · website; empty parts left out). Line format: `# Name`, `> Headline`, `@ contact`, `## Section`, `### Title, Place | Dates`, `- bullet`, plain lines, `**bold**`. `buildDiff` keeps the line marker when an edit drops it and adds confirmed skills after a `Label:` in the skills line. Old resumes (plain lines) still render; re-upload converts them. Students with no resume (or who want a better one) get **Make it with Claude** on the upload card: download `public/resume-with-claude.md` (a full Morning-Espresso-structure prompt: how to use it, purpose, inputs, step-by-step workflow with up to 8 questions first, rules per part, critical rules, tone, bad vs good from a made-up student, the exact template as OUTPUT FORMAT, example input), attach it in Claude with their old resume, answer Claude's questions, and upload the `resume.md` Claude returns (.md/.txt/.pdf are accepted). "Open Claude" and "Copy it instead" sit next to Download.

## Prompts

All prompts in `prompts/` follow one structure at the detail level of Rajveer's "Morning Espresso" example: title line `*SYSTEM PROMPT: …*`, purpose, `INPUTS` (every field explained), step-by-step `WORKFLOW` where each step says what it MUST include, a final self-check step, the rules for each output, `CRITICAL RULES`, `TONE`, example lines, `BAD vs GOOD` examples, `OUTPUT FORMAT` (exact JSON), and an example test input. Keep that structure when editing or adding one.

## Design rules (v2) — keep to these

Design source: the "Hunting Fox v2" canvas artifact. Tokens are in `src/app/globals.css` `:root`.

- Light mode only. Font Reddit Sans, weights 400/600/700 only. Squircles (`corner-shape: squircle`) everywhere. iOS line spinner (`Spinner`).
- Brand: **dark-to-light blue gradient** `linear-gradient(135deg,#0a2a6b,#2a74e6)`, white text, white fox (`/fox-white.png`). Only on **one main button per screen** (the student's real next step) and the logo tile. **No yellow, no orange, no pink.** Never a background.
- Sub text is **grey** `#5b6472` (`var(--mute)`). Ink `#101828`. Page `#f2f4f7`, cards white, sunken `#f7f8fa`, fill `#e7eaee`, tile `#eef1f5`.
- Selected state = ink fill + white + ✓. Company/person tiles are neutral; the only colour in a row is its status tag.
- Status: success `#1a6b3c/#e5f3e9`, danger `#b8132f/#fdecef`, info `#164a88/#e7effa`, waiting/neutral `#5b6472/#eef1f5`, warning (real warnings only) `#4f3e00/#ffefb0` with ⚠.
- Type: display clamp(36,6vw,48) · h1 clamp(30,4vw,36) · h2 24 · title 18 · body 16 · caption 14 · tag 13 · label 12 caps.
- Buttons: 36 / 44 / 52 tall; 44 minimum on touch.
- Every loading action shows a spinner immediately. `PressFeedback` (mounted in the root layout) does this for **every** button automatically: any `fetch` started within 400 ms of a click puts `data-busy` on that button (spinner, no double taps) until it answers; buttons with their own state set `aria-busy` to opt out (`LinkBox`, sign-in). Link clicks show a thin top bar; every route has a `loading.tsx` skeleton (`PageLoading`).

- The top bar (`Header`) is sticky and carries the page's way back (`back={{href,label}}`, a white "‹ Back to {company}" pill next to the logo) on the job, resume and reach-out pages, so it's reachable without scrolling up. On phones the logo text and the "My jobs" text hide to make room.
- Lists have the Newest/Oldest toggle (`useSort` in `src/components/ui.tsx`).
